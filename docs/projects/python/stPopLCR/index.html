1. Introduction & Study Area

The Liverpool City Region (LCR) is a Combined Authority located in the North-West of the United Kingdom (53.402^\circ\text{N}, 2.977^\circ\text{W}), covering a land extent of approximately 726\text{ km}^2. It comprises six Local Authority Districts:

  1. Liverpool (E08000012) — the metropolitan core (31\% of the regional population)
  2. Wirral (E08000015) — peninsular district across the River Mersey (21\%)
  3. Sefton (E08000014) — coastal corridor extending north to Southport (18\%)
  4. St. Helens (E08000013) — eastern industrial and commuter district (12\%)
  5. Knowsley (E08000011) — suburban and industrial belt bordering Liverpool (10\%)
  6. Halton (E06000006) — Runcorn and Widnes straddling the upper Mersey estuary (8\%)

Together, the six districts support a residential population of over 1.5 million residents and more than 37,000 active businesses (Christie, 2013). In Geographic Information Science (GIScience), representing such complex socio-spatial systems requires distinguishing between where people live at night (Residential Lower Layer Super Output Areas, LSOAs) and where people work during the day (Workplace Zones, WZs), as well as tracking how population redistributes over multi-decadal timescales.

This study reproduces the complete GIS workflow originally conducted in QGIS using reproducible Python (GeoPandas, Rasterio, Matplotlib), examining:

  • Workplace (Day-Time) Population & Density (2011) alongside Major Employers (hospitals, ports, docks, universities, and airports).
  • Residential (Night-Time) Population & Density (2011) and potential daily commute corridors connecting high-density residential neighbourhoods to employment hubs.
  • Multi-Decadal Population Change (1991–2011) using 1\text{ km} \times 1\text{ km} gridded raster surfaces from the UK Data Service PopChange project.
  • Socio-Economic Structure (Approximated Social Grade) across Workplace Zones (Professional, Semi-Skilled, and Skilled Manual).

2. Understanding the Geospatial File Formats (.gpkg vs. .shp 6-File Sets)

All required spatial datasets for this assignment are stored in the project data repository at data/data_python/stPopLCR/. Two primary vector formats are used alongside ASCII raster grids (.asc):

  1. GeoPackage (.gpkg): An open OGC single-file SQLite container (LCR_boundaries.gpkg and LCR_LSOA2021.gpkg) that stores vector geometries, spatial indexes, Coordinate Reference System (CRS) definitions (EPSG:27700, OSGB 1936 / British National Grid), and untruncated attribute tables inside a single self-contained file.
  2. ESRI Shapefile (.shp + 5 companion files = 6 files per layer): Every boundary and thematic shapefile consists of six coordinated files sharing the same basename:
    • .shp — Primary feature geometry (polygons/points in British National Grid metres).
    • .shx — Positional shape index allowing fast record seeking.
    • .dbf — dBASE attribute table storing census counts, densities, and classifications.
    • .prj — Projection / CRS definition in Well-Known Text (OSGB36 / British National Grid).
    • .cpg — Character encoding specification (UTF-8).
    • .qmd — QGIS XML metadata and layer styling catalog.

def add_north_arrow(ax, x=0.88, y=0.88):
    """Draws a classic GIS north arrow."""
    ax.annotate(
        "N", xy=(x, y + 0.06), xytext=(x, y - 0.02),
        xycoords="axes fraction", textcoords="axes fraction",

def add_scale_bar(ax, x0=344000, y0=376500, seg_km=5, two_sided=True):
    """Draws an alternating black-and-white metric scale bar in British National Grid metres."""
    seg_m = seg_km * 1000
    h = 450
    if two_sided:
        ax.add_patch(mpatches.Rectangle((x0 - seg_m, y0), seg_m, h, facecolor="black", edgecolor="black", lw=0.8))
        ax.add_patch(mpatches.Rectangle((x0, y0), seg_m, h, facecolor="white", edgecolor="black", lw=0.8))
        ax.add_patch(mpatches.Rectangle((x0 + seg_m, y0), seg_m, h, facecolor="black", edgecolor="black", lw=0.8))
        ax.text(x0 - seg_m, y0 + h * 2.2, f"{seg_km}", ha="center", va="bottom", fontsize=9)
        ax.text(x0, y0 + h * 2.2, "0", ha="center", va="bottom", fontsize=9)
        ax.text(x0 + seg_m, y0 + h * 2.2, f"{seg_km}", ha="center", va="bottom", fontsize=9)

Workplace Zones (WZs) are output geographies designed by the Office for National Statistics (2012) by splitting and merging Census Output Areas so that each zone contains an average of approximately 400 workers and consistent business intensity. Because daytime employment counts exhibit a highly skewed range (113 to 5,754 workers per zone), Natural Breaks (Jenks) classification is applied across 6 classes (113–368, 368–555, 555–880, 880–1503, 1503–2583, and 2583–5754).

Overlaid in orange are the Major Employers extracted from Ordnance Survey VectorMap District FunctionalSite polygons located within high-employment Workplace Zones, including Port of Liverpool, Liverpool Docks, Liverpool John Lennon Airport, Liverpool John Moores University, Ship Ferry (Foot) Terminal, Stalbridge Dock, and major regional hospitals (University Hospital Aintree, Whiston Hospital, Arrowe Park Hospital, Clatterbridge Hospital, Broadgreen Hospital, Mossley Hill Hospital, St Helens Hospital, Ashworth (North) Hospital, Waterloo EMI Day Hospital, St Bartholomews Day Hospital, and Halton General Hospital).

5. Residential (Night-Time) Population & Density Analysis

5.1 Figure 3: Residential (Night-Time) Population Distribution (2011)

Residential Lower Layer Super Output Areas (LSOAs) are constructed so that each neighbourhood contains an average of 1,500 residents (typically 400 to 1,200 households). Across the Liverpool City Region, total residential population in 2011 is 1,506,940, compared with 987,210 in Workplace Zones — indicating a maximum difference of 519,730 residents (accounting for non-working dependents, retirees, students, and out-commuters).

Key Findings & Methodological Conclusions

  1. Major Employment Hubs: Daytime employment in the LCR is heavily anchored by major institutional and transport sites, including the Port of Liverpool, Liverpool Docks, Liverpool John Lennon Airport, Liverpool John Moores University, and major NHS hospitals (Arrowe Park, Whiston, Aintree, Broadgreen, St Helens, and Halton General).

Comparing high-density residential clusters (\text{Density} > 73\text{ persons/ha} in Sefton Park/Wavertree, Bebington, New Brighton/Egremont, and Stockbridge Village) with high-density workplace clusters (\text{Density} > 51\text{ workers/ha} in Liverpool City Centre and Birkenhead) highlights primary daily commuting bottlenecks along the Queensway Tunnel, Edge Lane, and Liverpool Road.
  3. Divergent Regional vs. National Demographic Trends (1991–2011): While the National Mean population density increased from 223.10 to 246.20\text{ persons/km}^2 between 1991 and 2011, the LCR Mean declined slightly from 2087.17 to 2065.31\text{ persons/km}^2 due to suburban and post-industrial population shrinkage across outer districts—strongly counterbalanced by intense urban core re-densification in Liverpool City Centre, Wavertree, Litherland, Sutton, New Boston, and Widnes.
  4. GIScience Limitations (MAUP & Classification Sensitivity): Direct overlay comparison between Daytime and Nighttime populations is constrained by the Modifiable Areal Unit Problem (MAUP) because Workplace Zones (431 in Liverpool) and Residential LSOAs (298 in Liverpool) have non-nested geometries. Furthermore, choropleth interpretation is sensitive to the choice of classification scheme (Jenks vs. Quantiles vs. Equal Interval).

## 1. Introduction & Study Area

The **Liverpool City Region (LCR)** is a Combined Authority located in the North-West of the United Kingdom ($53.402^\circ\text{N}, 2.977^\circ\text{W}$), covering a land extent of approximately $726\text{ km}^2$. It comprises six Local Authority Districts:

1. **Liverpool** (`E08000012`) — the metropolitan core ($31\%$ of the regional population)
2. **Wirral** (`E08000015`) — peninsular district across the River Mersey ($21\%$)
3. **Sefton** (`E08000014`) — coastal corridor extending north to Southport ($18\%$)
4. **St. Helens** (`E08000013`) — eastern industrial and commuter district ($12\%$)
5. **Knowsley** (`E08000011`) — suburban and industrial belt bordering Liverpool ($10\%$)
6. **Halton** (`E06000006`) — Runcorn and Widnes straddling the upper Mersey estuary ($8\%$)

Together, the six districts support a residential population of over **1.5 million residents** and more than **37,000 active businesses** (Christie, 2013). In Geographic Information Science (GIScience), representing such complex socio-spatial systems requires distinguishing between **where people live at night** (Residential Lower Layer Super Output Areas, LSOAs) and **where people work during the day** (Workplace Zones, WZs), as well as tracking how population redistributes over multi-decadal timescales.

This study reproduces the complete GIS workflow originally conducted in **QGIS** using reproducible Python (`GeoPandas`, `Rasterio`, `Matplotlib`), examining:

* **Workplace (Day-Time) Population & Density (2011)** alongside **Major Employers** (hospitals, ports, docks, universities, and airports).
* **Residential (Night-Time) Population & Density (2011)** and **potential daily commute corridors** connecting high-density residential neighbourhoods to employment hubs.
* **Multi-Decadal Population Change (1991–2011)** using $1\text{ km} \times 1\text{ km}$ gridded raster surfaces from the UK Data Service *PopChange* project.
* **Socio-Economic Structure (Approximated Social Grade)** across Workplace Zones (*Professional*, *Semi-Skilled*, and *Skilled Manual*).

## 2. Understanding the Geospatial File Formats (`.gpkg` vs. `.shp` 6-File Sets)

All required spatial datasets for this assignment are stored in the project data repository at `data/data_python/stPopLCR/`. Two primary vector formats are used alongside ASCII raster grids (`.asc`):

1. **GeoPackage (`.gpkg`)**: An open OGC single-file SQLite container (`LCR_boundaries.gpkg` and `LCR_LSOA2021.gpkg`) that stores vector geometries, spatial indexes, Coordinate Reference System (CRS) definitions (`EPSG:27700`, OSGB 1936 / British National Grid), and untruncated attribute tables inside a single self-contained file.
2. **ESRI Shapefile (`.shp` + 5 companion files = 6 files per layer)**: Every boundary and thematic shapefile consists of six coordinated files sharing the same basename:
   * `.shp` — Primary feature geometry (polygons/points in British National Grid metres).
   * `.shx` — Positional shape index allowing fast record seeking.
   * `.dbf` — dBASE attribute table storing census counts, densities, and classifications.
   * `.prj` — Projection / CRS definition in Well-Known Text (`OSGB36 / British National Grid`).
   * `.cpg` — Character encoding specification (`UTF-8`).
   * `.qmd` — QGIS XML metadata and layer styling catalog.

## 3. Data Sources & Cartographic Classification Methodology

| Thematic Analysis | Dataset & Source | Spatial Unit | Classification Method | Cartographic Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Day-Time Population** | CDRC 2011 Census Workplace Data Pack (`WP201EW`) & OS VectorMap District (`FunctionalSite`) | Workplace Zones (WZs) | **Natural Breaks (Jenks, 6 classes)** | Workplace counts have an extreme right-skewed range ($113\text{–}5,754$); Jenks minimises within-class variance and maximises between-class contrast. |
| **Day-Time Density** | CDRC 2011 Workplace Zones (`WorkPop / Area_ha`) | Workplace Zones (WZs) | **Equal Count (Quantiles, 5 classes)** | Treating density as an ordinal categorical variable (*Very Low* to *Very High*) allows direct rank-order comparison against residential density. |
| **Night-Time Population** | CDRC 2011 Census Residential Data Pack (`KS102EW`) | LSOAs (2011) | **Natural Breaks (Jenks, 6 classes)** | Captures skewed residential population counts ($988\text{–}3,552$) and isolates extreme institutional/student clusters ($\text{Pop} > 3,000$). |
| **Night-Time Density** | CDRC 2011 Residential LSOAs (`Pop2011 / Area_ha`) | LSOAs (2011) | **Equal Count (Quantiles, 5 classes)** | Ordinal quantile classification highlights top-tier residential concentrations ($\text{Density} > 73\text{ persons/ha}$) to trace commute flows. |
| **1991–2011 Pop Change** | UK Data Service *PopChange* $1\text{ km}$ Grids (`TP91Bs` & `URPopAll`) | $1\text{ km} \times 1\text{ km}$ Raster Cells | **Equal Interval (5 classes)** | Divides the net change range into ordinal growth/decline tiers (*Very Negative* to *Very Positive*) to pinpoint urban regeneration clusters. |
| **Social Grade** | CDRC 2011 Workplace Table `WP613EW` (Approximated Social Grade) | Workplace Zones (WZs) | **Equal Interval (3 classes)** | Partitions workplace zones into *Skilled Manual*, *Semi-Skilled*, and *Professional* occupational profiles. |

### Key Findings & Methodological Conclusions

1. **Major Employment Hubs:** Daytime employment in the LCR is heavily anchored by major institutional and transport sites, including the **Port of Liverpool**, **Liverpool Docks**, **Liverpool John Lennon Airport**, **Liverpool John Moores University**, and major NHS hospitals (**Arrowe Park**, **Whiston**, **Aintree**, **Broadgreen**, **St Helens**, and **Halton General**).
2. **Daytime vs. Nighttime Contrasts & Commute Patterns:** Total daytime workplace population across the LCR is **$987,210$** compared to a nighttime residential population of **$1,506,940$** (a difference of **$519,730$**, reflecting both non-working residents and net out-commuting). Comparing high-density residential clusters ($\text{Density} > 73\text{ persons/ha}$ in Sefton Park/Wavertree, Bebington, New Brighton/Egremont, and Stockbridge Village) with high-density workplace clusters ($\text{Density} > 51\text{ workers/ha}$ in Liverpool City Centre and Birkenhead) highlights primary daily commuting bottlenecks along the **Queensway Tunnel**, **Edge Lane**, and **Liverpool Road**.
3. **Divergent Regional vs. National Demographic Trends (1991–2011):** While the **National Mean** population density increased from **$223.10$** to **$246.20\text{ persons/km}^2$** between 1991 and 2011, the **LCR Mean** declined slightly from **$2087.17$** to **$2065.31\text{ persons/km}^2$** due to suburban and post-industrial population shrinkage across outer districts—strongly counterbalanced by intense urban core re-densification in **Liverpool City Centre**, **Wavertree**, **Litherland**, **Sutton**, **New Boston**, and **Widnes**.
4. **GIScience Limitations (MAUP & Classification Sensitivity):** Direct overlay comparison between Daytime and Nighttime populations is constrained by the **Modifiable Areal Unit Problem (MAUP)** because Workplace Zones (431 in Liverpool) and Residential LSOAs (298 in Liverpool) have non-nested geometries. Furthermore, choropleth interpretation is sensitive to the choice of classification scheme (Jenks vs. Quantiles vs. Equal Interval).
